"lyra": minor
---

Lyra tells people apart far more reliably, above all two or more people sharing one laptop mic. It now uses a different voice model (TitaNet, downloaded once, about 40 MB), and after a recording stops it regroups the whole session by voice in a new way. On recordings of real voices in rooms, hybrid meetings and calls, speakers are mixed up about a third as often as before. Saved voices made with the old model can't be compared with the new one, so name yourself once in a session and Lyra recognizes you again from then on.

A saved voice now holds a session together: if one person was split into two speakers and both clearly sound like your saved voice, they become one speaker. Lyra also learns a voice on its own only from a speaker who talked for a while and whom nobody else in the session sounds like, so a wrong guess no longer teaches it the wrong voice.

When you move a line to another speaker, Lyra offers to move that speaker's other lines that sound the same, so one correction fixes the rest.
